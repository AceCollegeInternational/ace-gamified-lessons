Reading Comprehension

Understanding what you read — literally AND between the lines

Key Skills:
📌 Main Idea — what the whole text is mostly about
📌 Key Details — facts that support the main idea
📌 Inference — using clues to figure out what the author doesn't say directly
📌 Author's Purpose — why did the author write this? (inform, entertain, persuade)
📄 Read This!

🌻 Maya's Garden

Maya had always loved growing things. Last spring, she dug up a small patch of soil in her backyard and planted tomato seeds. Every morning before school, she carefully watered her plants and pulled out any weeds. By summer, the tomatoes were fat and bright red. Maya picked them and shared them with her neighbors, who said they were the tastiest tomatoes they had ever eaten. Maya decided she would plant an even bigger garden next year.

🟢 Main Idea: Maya works hard to grow tomatoes and shares them.
🟡 Key Detail: She watered and weeded every morning.
🔵 Inference: Maya is dedicated and kind (we infer this from her actions).
🟠 Author's Purpose: To entertain and show that hard work pays off.

👆 Click cards to reveal answers!

How do you find the MAIN IDEA of a passage?
Ask: "What is this MOSTLY about?" — it covers the whole text, not just one detail. Look at the first and last sentences!
What is an INFERENCE?
An inference is a smart guess using text clues + what you already know. The author hints at it — you figure it out! 🔎
💡 Remember: Main idea ≠ first sentence. The first sentence is often a hook!

Vocabulary & Word Study

Context clues, prefixes, suffixes, synonyms & antonyms

Context Clues — use words AROUND an unknown word to figure out its meaning.
Prefixes — letters added to the FRONT of a word to change its meaning.
Suffixes — letters added to the END of a word.
Synonyms — words that mean the SAME (happy → joyful).
Antonyms — words that mean the OPPOSITE (hot → cold).
Prefix/SuffixMeaningExample
un-not / oppositeunhappy, unkind
re-againrewrite, replay
pre-beforepreview, prepay
-fulfull ofhopeful, joyful
-lesswithouthopeless, careless
-er / -estcomparisontaller, tallest
💡 Context Clue Trick: Cover the unknown word. Read the rest of the sentence. What word would make sense there? That's your clue! 🕵️

Grammar & Language

Parts of speech, punctuation & sentence structure

Parts of Speech Quick Guide:
Noun — person, place, thing, or idea  |  Verb — action or state of being
Adjective — describes a noun  |  Adverb — describes a verb, adj, or adverb
Preposition — shows relationship (in, on, under, beside)
The quick fox jumped swiftly over the lazy dog.
Sentence Types:
📌 Simple — one independent clause: The dog barked.
📌 Compound — two clauses joined by and/but/or: I like cats, but she prefers dogs.
📌 Complex — independent + dependent clause: Because it rained, we stayed inside.
💡 Apostrophe Trick: it's = it IS  |  its = belonging to it.
"The cat licked its paw." (belonging) vs. "It's a nice day." (it is)

Literary Elements

Character, setting, plot, theme, point of view & figurative language

Story Elements:
🧑 Character — who the story is about
🏡 Setting — where and when the story happens
📈 Plot — what happens (problem → events → solution)
💡 Theme — the big life lesson or message
👁️ Point of View — who is telling the story (1st, 2nd, or 3rd person)
🎭 Simile
"Her smile was like sunshine."
Compares using like or as
🎭 Metaphor
"Her laughter was music."
Direct comparison — no like/as
🎭 Personification
"The wind whispered secrets."
Gives human traits to non-humans
🎭 Alliteration
"Peter Piper picked peppers."
Same starting sound repeated
💡 Point of View: 1st person uses I/me/we. 3rd person uses he/she/they. Check the pronouns!

Q 2 of 3 Point of View · +10 pts

Read the passage opening:
"She ran as fast as her legs could carry her. The forest was dark, but she was not afraid." What is the point of view of this passage?

Circle the pronouns: "she" and "her." These are third-person pronouns. First person = I/me/we. Second = you/your.
🧠 Mini Quiz – Literary Elements

Writing & Text Structure

Main idea, text organization, opinion vs. fact & author's craft

Types of Writing:
📣 Opinion / Argument — writer's belief + reasons + evidence
📰 Informative / Explanatory — facts and information about a topic
🌟 Narrative — tells a story with characters, plot, and setting
📋 Text Structure Patterns:
📝 Cause & Effect — one event causes another (because, as a result, therefore)
📝 Compare & Contrast — similarities and differences (however, both, on the other hand)
📝 Sequence / Order — steps in order (first, next, then, finally)
📝 Problem & Solution — a problem is presented and solved
💡 Opinion vs. Fact:
FACT = can be proven true ✅ ("Dogs have four legs.")
OPINION = someone's belief 💭 ("Dogs are the best pets.")
🧠 Mini Quiz – Writing & Text Structure
Q 1 of 3 Opinion vs Fact · +10 pts

Which sentence is an OPINION?

An opinion is someone's BELIEF — it cannot be proven true or false. Which sentence uses a judgment word like "best"?

Read the paragraph:
"Because it rained heavily all week, the river overflowed its banks. As a result, several families had to leave their homes temporarily." What text structure is this?

The signal words "Because" and "As a result" are strong clues. These words show that one event LEADS TO another event.
