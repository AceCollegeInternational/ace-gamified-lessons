Multi-Digit Multiplication

4-digit × 1-digit and 2-digit × 2-digit using the standard algorithm

Standard Algorithm — 3 steps:
1️⃣ Multiply ones digit × each digit of top number
2️⃣ Write a 0, then multiply tens digit × each digit
3️⃣ ADD the two partial products together
📐 Standard Algorithm:   36 × 24
Step 1: 36 × 4 = 144 (ones partial product)
Step 2: 36 × 20 = 720 (tens partial product — write a 0!)
Step 3: 144 + 720 = 864 ✅
Why write a zero when multiplying by the tens digit?
Because you're multiplying by TENS (20, not 2). The zero holds the ones place. 20 × 3 = 60, not 6! 🎯
💡 Estimation check: 36 × 24 ≈ 40 × 25 = 1000. Our answer 864 is close — makes sense!

Division with Remainders

Long division · interpreting remainders in context

Division parts:
Dividend ÷ Divisor = Quotient R Remainder
Check: (Quotient × Divisor) + Remainder = Dividend ✅
🚨 Interpreting Remainders:
📌 Round UP — "How many bags needed?" (you need an extra bag for leftovers)
📌 Round DOWN — "How many FULL groups?" (leftover doesn't make a full group)
📌 Use the remainder — "How many left over?"
23 students go on a trip. Each car holds 4 students. How many cars do they need?
23÷4 = 5 R3. They need 5 full cars + 1 more for the 3 remaining = 6 cars. Round UP! 🚗

Mixed Numbers: A whole number + a fraction
2¾ = 2 + ¾ = 11/4 (improper fraction)
Comparing: Same denominator → compare numerators.
Different denominator → convert to same denominator first!
¾ vs ⅝ → 6/8 vs 5/8 → ¾ is greater!
How do you compare 2/3 and 3/4?
Find a common denominator: 2/3=8/12, 3/4=9/12. Compare: 8/12 < 9/12, so 3/4 is greater! 🎯

Angles & Geometry

Measuring angles · Types of angles · Lines & symmetry

Angle Types:
🔹 Acute — less than 90° (like a sharp pizza slice)
🔹 Right — exactly 90° (perfect corner ⊾)
🔹 Obtuse — between 90° and 180°
🔹 Straight — exactly 180° (flat line)
🔹 A full turn = 360°
Angle addition:
If two angles share a side and together form a right angle:
35° + ? = 90° → ? = 90° − 35° = 55°
💡 Line types: Parallel lines (never meet) || · Perpendicular lines (meet at 90°) ⊥ · Intersecting lines (cross at any angle) ✕
